Cervical health screening requires two important checkpoints: HPV testing is responsible for detecting "external invasion," while the CISCER® PAX1/JAM3 methylation test focuses on identifying the cell's own "internal transformation." The two complement each other, but methylation testing can more directly reveal the "internal malignant transformation signals" as cells progress toward lesions — and can sometimes even gain early insight into cervical cancer before other methods!

**Checkpoint 1: HPV Testing — Detecting "Enemy Traces"**

1. What Does It Test For?

HPV testing specifically looks for traces of cancer-causing "high-risk" human papillomavirus (HPV) types (viral DNA or RNA). Simply put, it checks whether dangerous viruses (especially the most dangerous types) have entered your cervix.

2. What Do the Results Mean?

(1) Positive (+): High-risk virus detected! This is the main "culprit" of cervical cancer. But remember: the vast majority of people (about 90%) have strong immune "defenders" that can clear the virus within 1–2 years.

(2) Negative (-): Good news! There's likely no high-risk enemy present, significantly reducing risk. However, even the wisest can occasionally miss something;

No test is "all-seeing." HPV testing also has "escapees" (false negatives)! For example: the enemy is present in very small numbers (extremely low viral load), the lesion was not "caught" during biopsy sampling, or the patient is infected with a high-risk type not covered by current testing (HPV generally has over 200 types, with testing focusing on 14 types). There is no 100% perfectly accurate testing technology in the world.

**Checkpoint 2: CISCER® PAX1/JAM3 Methylation Testing — Detecting "Internal Transformation"**

1. What Does It Test For?

Rather than looking for the virus, it directly probes inside our cervical cells! The CISCER® PAX1/JAM3 methylation test specifically checks whether key "safety guardian" genes (PAX1, JAM3) within cells have been "sealed" (hypermethylated). These genes are normally responsible for keeping cells from growing uncontrollably. Once "sealed" (hypermethylated), cells may "lose control" and embark on a path of rapid high-grade lesion progression.

2. Why Is It Important?

The CISCER® methylation test provides dual safety signals from PAX1 and JAM3 — essentially directly monitoring "danger signals" emitted from within cells! It can earlier and more directly indicate whether cells have undergone substantive molecular changes closely associated with the progression of precancerous lesions (especially high-grade CIN2/3). Scrutinizing the subtle to understand the significant!

3. What Do the Results Mean?

- Positive (+): This suggests that serious "mutiny" or loss of control may have occurred inside cells (hypermethylation), highly correlated with high-grade precancerous lesions (CIN2/3) and even risk of cancer progression. This is a strong signal reflecting the actual progression status of the disease!
- Negative (-): The cell's interior is currently relatively "stable." Key "safety guardian" genes are still functioning, and the risk of rapid deterioration in the short term is very low — an important "reassurance."

Two checkpoints, different result combinations call for different management strategies!

1. Scenario 1: HPV Positive (+) + Methylation Negative (-)

Next steps: Follow physician's advice. If positive for high-risk HPV 16/18, colposcopy is still required; for other high-risk types with normal cervical cytology (TCT), HPV + methylation testing can be repeated in 6 months.

2. Scenario 2: HPV Negative (-) + Methylation Positive (+)

Persistent high-risk HPV infection is typically the "mastermind" causing cellular "internal transformation" (methylation positivity). Therefore, HPV-negative but methylation-positive, while uncommon, is like "alarm lights flashing wildly" — requiring heightened vigilance!

Possible causes:
- 1. The virus is hiding too deep:
Lesional cells are hiding in the endocervical canal or only a small amount of HPV infection was sampled (low viral load), cleverly evading HPV detection (false negative). But the infecting virus is sufficient to "fan the flames," triggering malignant transformation within cells! What's next?
- 2. Before colposcopy, the non-invasive CISCER® methylation test has already been performed. When positive, it alerts the colposcopist to pay special attention to lesions hiding deep within, to catch the concealed lesion.
- 3. No time to lose! Colposcopy + biopsy (histopathological examination) must be performed immediately! This is the "gold standard" for diagnosis. Magnified examination of the cervical surface + tissue sampling is necessary for accurate diagnosis and risk exclusion.
- 4. The longer you wait, the higher the risk. Regardless of whether you've been vaccinated against HPV, regardless of whether you have symptoms, regardless of whether your previous TCT results were normal — you must get tested ASAP!

Why the urgency?
- An HPV-negative result may seem like "all is peaceful," but a positive methylation result is like discovering "the inner courtyard is already on fire"!
- Methylation testing is like a guiding light, directing colposcopy + biopsy (the "demon-revealing mirror") in the right direction. Any potentially hidden serious problem must be uncovered — nipping it in the bud, without delay!
