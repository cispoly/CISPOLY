**01 The "Pain Points" of Traditional Screening: Like Seeing Through Fog**

Currently, high-risk human papillomavirus (hr-HPV) testing is the preferred method for cervical cancer screening, and cervical exfoliative cytology serves as a triage method for HPV-positive patients.

HPV testing checks for infection with high-risk human papillomavirus (the main "culprit" of cervical cancer). But it has a problem: it is extremely sensitive to the presence of high-risk viruses but cannot distinguish between "transient visitors" that will be cleared by the immune system and "lingering guests" that may progress to lesions. This leads to a relatively high false-positive rate, causing unnecessary worry. Many people can clear the virus on their own after infection (over 90%). So while HPV testing has high sensitivity, it struggles to distinguish "passersby" from "stayers." This easily causes "false alarms" and unnecessary worry.

Cervical smear/cytology (TCT/LCT): Physicians examine cervical cells under a microscope for lesions. But it heavily depends on the pathologist's judgment ability — very reliant on the physician's "eye for detail." In primary care hospitals with limited experience and equipment, it's like "a blind person fishing" — over 30% of CIN2+ (high-grade lesions) may be missed or misdiagnosed, leading to overtreatment or anxiety. So cytology results have problems of both "making a mountain out of a molehill" (unnecessary examinations) and "escapees" (missed diagnoses).
