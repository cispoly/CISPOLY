May 8th is World Ovarian Cancer Day. Ovarian cancer is known as the "king of gynecologic cancers"—due to its insidious early symptoms, approximately 70% of patients are already at intermediate to advanced stages at diagnosis, with a five-year survival rate below 50%. Early detection has become the key to fighting this disease.

![](./images/contents/世界卵巢癌日聚禾生物以甲基化技术助力更早发现沉默杀手-img-01.png)

**I. Pay Attention to Body Signals and Detect Risks Early**

The ovaries are located deep within the pelvis, making early lesions difficult to detect. The following symptoms warrant particular vigilance: persistent abdominal bloating and pain, decreased appetite with increased abdominal girth, frequent urination and urgency, changes in bowel habits, and unexplained fatigue or weight loss.

While these symptoms are not necessarily indicative of ovarian cancer, they should be taken seriously if persistent. The core challenge in ovarian cancer prevention and treatment lies in how to identify risk earlier, before symptoms become apparent.

The NMPA Class III-certified non-invasive ovarian cancer CDO1/HOXA9 methylation detection achieves early "molecular foresight" by capturing specific methylation signals in blood, providing women with a non-invasive and convenient screening option.

World Ovarian Cancer Day reminds us that paying attention to ovarian cancer is not only about one disease—it is about the long-term health of every woman. With the development of molecular detection technology, early diagnosis of ovarian cancer is undergoing breakthroughs.
