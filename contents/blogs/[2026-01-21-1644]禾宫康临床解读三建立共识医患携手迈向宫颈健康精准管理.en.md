Cervical cancer prevention and treatment is a shared journey that requires both professional medical judgment and active patient participation. The introduction of the CISCER® (PAX1/JAM3) gene methylation test provides a more precise "navigator" for this path. To fully realize its value, consensus between physicians and patients must be established at key junctures to form a collaborative force.

![](./images/contents/禾宫康临床解读三建立共识医患携手迈向宫颈健康精准管理-img-01.png)

**Consensus 1: On the Value of Testing — From "Detecting the Virus" to "Foreseeing the Risk"**

- ·**Physician's perspective**: This is a powerful **molecular pathology tool**. It goes beyond HPV testing (which only indicates "presence or absence of infection") by directly probing whether cells have initiated the molecular program toward precancerous lesions (HSIL), achieving a leap from "infection screening" to "**disease risk screening**." Its core value lies in **precise triage**, effectively distinguishing between "carrier status requiring no excessive intervention" and "progression risk that demands close monitoring or intervention."

- ·**Patient's perspective**: This is a clearer, personalized "**risk report**." When an HPV-positive or inconclusive TCT result causes anxiety, it can tell me whether my cervical cells are currently "essentially safe" or "require further examination," helping me understand why follow-up or colposcopy is needed, making my decisions more evidence-based, and reducing unnecessary panic and overtreatment.

- ·**Shared goal**: We agree that the core value of this test is to provide **earlier, more objective risk stratification for "high-grade" lesions**, focusing limited medical resources and attention on truly high-risk populations while reassuring those at low risk.

 **Consensus 2: On Timing of Testing — Precise Application at Key Decision Points**

Both parties should jointly understand that at the following critical clinical decision "crossroads," this test can provide key information:

1\.**During HPV-positive triage**: Especially for non-16/18 type positivity, consensus supports testing to determine whether immediate colposcopy is needed or whether safe follow-up is appropriate.

2\.**When TCT results are inconclusive**: When cytology reports show ASC-US or LSIL, consensus supports using it as an important adjunctive decision-making tool to clarify the risk level.

3\.**When screening special populations**: For postmenopausal women, immunosuppressed individuals, and others for whom conventional screening may be limited, consensus supports using it as an important supplement.

4\.**During post-treatment follow-up monitoring**: For patients who have completed treatment for high-grade cervical lesions, consensus supports using it as a more specific follow-up tool than HPV to provide earlier warning of recurrence risk.

**Consensus 3: On Result Interpretation and Action — Outcome-Oriented, Clear Path Forward**

This is the most critical aspect of physician-patient communication. Clear consensus can eliminate misunderstandings and facilitate optimal decision-making.

| **Test Result** | **Clinical Significance Consensus** | **Follow-up Action Consensus** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Methylation negative** | **Current risk is low**: Indicates that no definitive high-grade lesion signals have been detected at the molecular level in cervical cells. Even if HPV is positive, it suggests that the risk of near-term progression to high-grade lesions is very low. | **Reassurance with regular follow-up**: Patients can significantly alleviate anxiety. Physician and patient agree on follow-up at routine screening intervals (e.g., 1 year), typically without immediate colposcopy. This avoids overtreatment. |
| **Methylation positive** | **A signal requiring definitive diagnosis**: Indicates that stable molecular changes associated with high-grade precancerous lesions have appeared in cells. A "warning" has been detected that warrants — and justifies — pathological confirmation, requiring further examination. | Proceed actively with colposcopy: This is a necessary and proactive step. Physician and patient agree to arrange colposcopy (± biopsy) as soon as possible to clarify the specific degree of lesion. This is precisely the optimal intervention window to block cancer development; the vast majority of problems can be cured at this stage through intervention (e.g., minimally invasive treatment or minor outpatient procedures). |

**Consensus 4: On Communication and Decision-Making — Evidence-Based Shared Decision-Making**

- ·**Physician's responsibility**: Based on evidence-based medicine, clearly explain the test's principles, value, limitations, and the clinical pathways corresponding to different results, providing professional recommendations.

- ·**Patient's role**: Actively understand your own situation, be aware of the pros and cons of different options, and communicate fully with your physician, incorporating your own values and preferences.

- ·**Shared decision-making**: Before and after testing, both parties — based on this objective molecular evidence (methylation test result), combined with HPV, TCT results, and personal circumstances — jointly discuss and decide on the most appropriate individualized management plan — whether reassured follow-up or active intervention.

**Summary**

The CISCER® methylation test builds a communication bridge between physicians and patients based on molecular pathology risk evidence for high-grade cervical lesions. By establishing the above consensus, we can translate advanced technology into efficient clinical practice and reassuring personal health management. It ultimately points toward a shared goal:

**On the path of cervical cancer prevention and treatment, use more precise judgment to reduce unnecessary worry and treatment; use earlier detection to firmly seize the curable window that is entirely within our control.**
