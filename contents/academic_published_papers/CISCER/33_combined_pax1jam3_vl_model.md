## Abstract

**Background:** Persistent high-risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV) infection is the main cause of cervical cancer. However, most hrHPV infections are transient and do not progress to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia grade 2 or worse (CIN2+). Identifying women at high risk of CIN2+ among those with positive screening results is crucial for reducing unnecessary colposcopy referrals and optimizing clinical management.

**Methods:** This study included women with positive cervical screening results who underwent colposcopy and histopathological examination. PAX1/JAM3 methylation levels were detected by methylation-specific PCR, and HPV viral load was quantified. Multivariable logistic regression was used to develop a combined risk prediction model for CIN2+.

**Results:** The combined PAX1/JAM3 methylation and HPV viral load model showed good discriminatory ability for identifying CIN2+ among women with positive screening results. The model may help stratify women into different risk groups, potentially reducing unnecessary colposcopy referrals in low-risk women.

**Conclusions:** A combined PAX1/JAM3 methylation and HPV viral load model shows promise for risk stratification of CIN2+ among women with positive cervical screening results. Further validation in larger, independent cohorts is needed.
